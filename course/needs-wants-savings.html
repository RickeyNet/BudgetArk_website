⚖️ Needs, Wants, Savings (50/30/20)

The simplest budget framework in the world. Three buckets, three percentages.

  • 4 min read
  • Budgeting

WHY THIS MATTERS

If you only ever learn one budgeting rule, learn this one. It tells you the shape a healthy month should have.

The 50/30/20 rule splits your take-home pay into three buckets: 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings or debt payoff. It is the cheapest budget framework that still works.

Needs (50%)

  • Rent or mortgage, utilities, basic groceries
  • Transportation to work
  • Insurance and minimum debt payments
  • Anything you can't skip without real consequences

Wants (30%)

  • Restaurants, streaming, hobbies
  • Gym memberships you actually use
  • Travel, gifts, anything optional
  • If skipping it would feel sad but not catastrophic, it lives here

Savings + debt payoff (20%)

  • Emergency fund contributions
  • Retirement contributions
  • Any payment above the minimum on a debt
  • Sinking funds for car repair, holidays, anything future

USE TAKE-HOME, NOT GROSS

The percentages apply to what hits your bank account, after taxes and pre-tax deductions. If your paycheck shows $4,000, that's the number to split.

Almost no one hits 50/30/20 on the first try. Rent in a big city alone can eat 50% before you've bought a single grocery. That's fine. The percentages are guardrails, not laws. Use them to see where you are, not where you have to be.

If wants are 50% and savings is 0%, the framework is telling you something. Listen, then decide what to do about it. Don't punish yourself, just move one number at a time.

KEY TAKEAWAY

50% needs, 30% wants, 20% future. Start there. Tune later.
