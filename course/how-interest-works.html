📈 How interest actually works

Interest is rent on borrowed money. The APR tells you how expensive that rent is per year.

  • 6 min read
  • Debt

WHY THIS MATTERS

Minimum payments are designed to keep you paying for years. Understanding interest is how you break that loop.

APR (annual percentage rate) is what the lender charges per year on the balance you still owe. It is not a one-time fee. Every month, interest accrues on whatever balance is left, then your payment covers interest first and only then chips away at principal.

What that means in practice

  • Higher APR = more of each payment disappears into interest
  • A $5,000 card at 24% APR costs over $1,000 a year in interest if the balance never drops
  • Paying only the minimum often leaves the balance barely moving

THE MINIMUM PAYMENT TRAP

Minimums are built to keep accounts profitable for the lender. They are not a payoff plan. If you only pay the minimum on high-rate debt, you are renting that balance indefinitely.

Compound interest works against you on debt: interest earns interest on the next cycle. That is why knocking down the highest-rate balance first (avalanche) saves real money, and why even an extra $50 above the minimum changes the timeline.

KEY TAKEAWAY

APR is the price of waiting. Pay more than the minimum on the most expensive balance when you can.
