November 10, 2026 v1.11.2

Nothing Fails Quietly

  • Refunds are no longer counted as income. When money comes back for something you returned, tick 'This is a refund' on the deposit in the Review Inbox and pick the category the purchase was in. It lowers that category's spending for the month and stays out of your income. Refunds you already filed as income can be fixed the same way: open the entry and tick the box. If you have an 'always' rule for a store, money coming back from that store is filed as a refund for you.
  • Paychecks update your income line instead of adding new ones. When you file a bank deposit as someone's paycheck, the Budget tab used to add a second income row under the bank's long description next to the one you set up. Now that person's income stays one tidy line under the name you gave it, showing what has been deposited so far plus the estimate for the paychecks still to come, with how many are in. Tap the line to see each deposit by date and to edit the monthly estimate. The totals are the same as before - only the list is cleaner.
  • Payments you receive can be assigned to a business. A deposit in the Review Inbox now has the same Business picker an expense does, and so does income you add by hand. Tick 'always' and future payments from that client are tagged for you. The business report shows each business's income next to its expenses with the net, and its CSV export includes the income rows.
  • A business only adds to your income when it makes money. Once you assign income to a business, your budget counts just that business's profit for the month - what it took in minus what it spent - and nothing in a month it runs at a loss. Its own bills no longer count as your personal spending. The income box shows one line per business that opens to its entries, and the same rule applies to cash flow, charts, insights, limits and the annual report. A business you only tag expenses to works exactly as before.
  • Running a business by hand is simpler. You can now create a business right from the Add Entry form - no trip to Profile first - and each business's line in the Budget tab's income box opens with '+ Income' and '+ Expense' buttons that start an entry already assigned to it. Logging the real charge for a business bill, or filing an entry against a business's recurring income or bill, now carries the business over automatically.
  • Rental properties get tenants. Under Profile → Businesses, tick Rental property when you add a business (or tap Edit on one you already have), then tap Tenants on it to add who rents from you and their monthly rent. Other businesses stay as they were, with no tenant options. Rent is added to what each tenant owes every month on its due day, and any income you assign to the tenant - by hand, from a bank deposit in the Review Inbox, or with Log payment - pays it down. A short payment simply leaves the rest owing, month by month, and a tenant who pays ahead carries a credit. You can raise the rent from a chosen month, record rent already owed, add a late fee or a one-off charge such as a repair, and mark a tenant as moved out. A tenant who started in the past is quick to set up: enter the month they moved in and the last month they paid in full, and a payment is added for every month in between. Add past payments on a tenant's card does the same for a tenant you already have. Months, dates and the rent due day in the tenant forms are picked from a calendar instead of typed. This is separate from Owed to You, which stays for money you have lent.
  • Loans can be removed from Owed to You. Each loan there now has a Remove link: choose Delete expense to take it out of your budget as well, or Keep expense to leave it as an ordinary expense and only clear it from the list.
  • Approved transactions can be changed without leaving the Review Inbox. A new Recently approved row sits next to Recently skipped and lists the bank transactions approved in the last 30 days - by you, by one of your rules, or on your partner's phone. Tap one to fix its name, category, people or business and save; the entry in your budget updates right away. Transactions you logged as a debt payment or added to a purchase plan are not listed there.
  • Nothing fails quietly anymore. If a debt payment can't be saved, the amount you typed stays in the box with a note instead of vanishing as if it had been recorded - and that note only appears when the payment truly wasn't saved, so it never nudges you into paying twice. Adding, editing or deleting a debt that can't be saved now says so and keeps the form or the confirmation open so you can try again, instead of doing nothing; the same goes for an Undo that can't be applied. A bank sync started from the Review Inbox now tells you when it failed or when a bank needs attention. A currency conversion that stops partway says so, and warns you that some amounts may already be converted. And a partner sync that breaks shows Sync Failed instead of simply going back to idle.
  • Encrypted backups are harder to get wrong. Exporting one now asks for the password twice and lets you show what you typed, so a typo can no longer lock you out of your own backup - and if the two don't match or the password is too short, the dialog says so right under the field instead of popping a second box over it. Importing one asks for the file once: a wrong password shows right in the prompt and you can try again, instead of starting over from the file picker, and the Decrypt & Import button shows a spinner while the backup decrypts.
  • Reset All Data tells you everything it removes. The warning now names receipt photos and the automatic backups stored on this device alongside your debts, payments and accounts, notes that receipt photos aren't part of a backup, and offers an Export a Backup First button before you commit.
  • Deleting a Bridge account takes two steps. Delete used to sit beside Cancel and Save and acted on a single tap. It is now a quiet link below them, and it asks first - naming the account, how many holdings go with it, and that the deletion reaches a paired partner. Afterwards an Undo button appears for a few seconds, just like deleting a debt or a budget entry, and brings the account and its holdings back. And if the delete can't be saved, you're told so instead of being left looking at an editor that did nothing.
  • The 'Open the calculator' buttons in lessons open the calculator. In the four lessons that point to a Charts tool, the button only closed the lesson. It now takes you to the Charts tab with the right calculator opened and scrolled into view.
  • The debt due prompt's mute button says what it does. 'Not yet this month' sounded like 'I still need to pay' but actually silenced the reminder for the month; it now reads 'Don't remind me this month'. It works exactly as before.
  • A fresh install no longer opens What's New over the welcome tour. New users saw the release notes pop up on top of the Bridge tour right after onboarding; they now start with the tour alone.
  • Buttons and checkmarks on the accent color are readable in every theme. White text on the accent color was hard to read in eight of the fifteen themes - Forest Gold and Lighthouse worst of all - starting with the Next button on the very first onboarding screen. Every button, chip, badge and checkmark that sits on the accent color now uses each theme's own button text color. The red and amber confirm buttons - Delete, Reset, Unpair, Replace - got the same treatment: their labels were faint in most themes and are now dark or light to suit each theme's red and amber. Green buttons - Merge, Save, confirm payment - follow the same rule.
  • The clock, signal and battery icons at the top of the screen are visible in the light themes. They stayed white on Ark Parchment, Rose, Chart Room, Harbor Dawn and Ledger, where they all but disappeared; they now turn dark on light themes and stay light on dark ones, switching as soon as you change theme.
  • Colored text is readable on cards in every theme. Green, amber, red and teal figures - and links in the accent color - were too pale to read in Rose, and the red was too dim in Slate, Easy, Synthwave, Coral and Forest Gold. Those colors are now deeper in the light themes and brighter in the dark ones. Rose changes the most: its pastels are now richer tones. Neon Purple's accent is a shade lighter.
  • Small grey text is easier to read in every theme. Captions, hints and placeholder text were too faint to read comfortably in almost every theme; they are now noticeably stronger on cards and on the background, while still sitting quieter than the main text. Lighthouse and 1998 (formerly Classic) already met the bar and look the same.
  • The Classic theme is now called 1998, and its yellow, green and teal text is readable. If you had Classic selected you still do - only the name changed. Warnings and notices in it were a dark yellow that nearly disappeared on the grey cards, and the green and teal weren't much better. All three are now deeper shades that stand out clearly, while the theme keeps its Windows 98 look.
  • Entries inside a spending category are easier to tap. Each entry in an expanded category was only as tall as its text, so it was easy to hit the one above or below. The rows are now full-height tap targets with room around them, and a selected row is highlighted with some breathing space.
  • Setup no longer runs straight into the guided tour. The last setup step now asks whether you want the tab-by-tab tour and says how many tips it holds, and Skip Setup skips the tour too. Every tip is still in Profile → Help → Feature guide.
  • Replacing your data now keeps a way back. Before a Replace import or a backup restore overwrites what is on the phone, BudgetArk saves a safety copy in Automatic Backups, and stops without changing anything if that copy can't be saved.
  • Quick actions can be taken back. A payment typed on a debt card and a savings change in the milestones sheet now offer Undo, and the Review Inbox asks before approving or skipping a whole batch.
  • The account editor tells you what's wrong. Saving an account with a missing name, a bad balance or an incomplete holding used to do nothing, and a holding with a field cleared could be removed without warning. Save now points at each field that needs fixing and changes nothing until they are right.
  • A tab that can't read your data says so. If the Debts, Budget or Bridge tab fails to load, it now shows a notice with Try again instead of looking empty. Nothing is deleted when this happens.
  • Setup starts by asking for your language. The first step lists every language under its own name, with Automatic still following your phone, and the rest of setup switches the moment you pick one. Three leftover English bits are translated too: the screen shown when the app can't read its data at launch, the footnote under the Automatic Backups list, and the screen-reader label on the Undo bar's close button.
  • The Debts tab gets to your debts sooner, and its shortcuts go where you expect. Tapping the payment-due banner before the due day now takes you to that debt with the payment box open, instead of opening the Edit Debt form. Payment History has a Select button for deleting payments - before, the only way was a long press nobody would guess. And the debt-free countdown moved below your debt list, so the first debt shows up without scrolling.
  • Buttons fit in German and Russian on small phones. Rows of three buttons - Back, Skip and Next in setup and the guided tour, Delete, Cancel and Save on a budget entry, the milestone step actions, and the restore choices in Automatic Backups - cut off their longer labels. They now size to their text and wrap onto a second line when they must, and so does the bottom row of a debt card.
  • Update pop-ups are shorter. The Update Ready and What's New messages now show just a one-line headline for each change instead of the full write-up. The full details are still there when you want them: the Update Ready message has links that open the complete list in your browser before you install, on the BudgetArk website or on GitHub, whichever you prefer, and it is always under Profile → Release Notes afterwards.
  • Only one Update Ready message appears. Tapping Check for Updates while the app was already downloading an update in the background could show two Update Ready messages stacked on top of each other. There is now a single update check and a single message.

Cards, Connected

  • A shorter Profile tab, with nothing taken away. People is one row: open it to manage the household, and the Person Spending Report and Owed to You sit at the top of that sheet. Businesses is one row the same way, with the Business Expense Report inside. Help is one row - the Feature guide - which now also holds the tab-by-tab tour that used to live under Onboarding (one search box covers both), an Update spotlight link at the top to rewatch what's new, and the Redo onboarding button in its footer. And Settings folds up: the card shows Check for Updates (tap it to check now; the Auto on / Auto off pill beside it switches automatic checks) and a More settings row that drops the rest down.
  • The Profile tab keeps getting shorter: thirteen section headers are now eight. Your name card carries the Ship's Log line, so Progress no longer needs its own card. Categories, People and Businesses sit together under one Organize header. Send Feedback and the Tip Jar keep their spot right under your name. Automatic Backups joined the Export & Backups sheet next to the encrypted backup and the spreadsheet, with its weekly / last-run status right there. Bank Connections shows how many transactions are waiting in the Review Inbox on its own line, so the Profile row that only jumped to the Budget tab is gone. And Partner Sync is one Sync Now row - your partner's name and the last sync underneath, an Auto on / Auto off pill once a home Wi-Fi is set, and a ⋯ button for Home Wi-Fi and Unpair, which no longer need standing rows.
  • Paychecks now adjust the plan instead of piling on top of it. A recurring income can name its earner (anyone under Profile → People) and how often they're paid - weekly, every 2 weeks, twice a month or monthly. When a deposit lands, the Review Inbox asks whose paycheck it is (and remembers the answer for that payroll merchant), and adding income by hand offers the same choice. The month then blends what has been deposited with the estimate for the paychecks still to come: after the first of two checks, half the estimate still counts, and once they've all arrived the month shows what was really paid. The income list says how many paychecks are in, and editing the earner's income shows what recent full months averaged with a one-tap update. Shift workers whose hours change every pay period finally get a budget that follows the real deposits without collapsing to the first one. The per-person report under Profile → People now shows each earner's income beside their spending, with a net line and an income total for the year, and its CSV gains a Type column.
  • Connected credit cards track their own balance - and now you can set that up where you connect the bank. A card on the Debts tab only follows the bank once it is linked to the matching bank account, and until now the only place to do that was inside the card's editor, easy to miss. The bank connection wizard and Profile → Bank Connections now offer a Cards on Debts choice right next to the Bridge accounts, point out accounts that look like credit cards, and push the bank's balance onto the card the moment you pick it. One account feeds one card, and a card never doubles as a Bridge balance.
  • The card keep-alive watch finally shows its work. A watched card keeps its status dot even while collapsed (green when all is fine), the expanded card says when it was last used and whether a connected bank account stamps that for you, the card editor shows a live 'last used · next use by' line as you change the window, and a card linked to the bank with Balance from bank turned off says so instead of looking unlinked. Switching the watch back on after a break restarts the clock today instead of reviving a months-old date, and a long-overdue card keeps getting its weekly reminder instead of going quiet after four weeks.
  • Bank sync keeps your balances moving even when the Review Inbox hiccups. If importing transactions fails partway through a sync, the pass still updates your Bridge and card balances, and the connection shows the error in Profile → Bank Connections instead of silently stalling.
  • Bank connections tell you what's going on. Sync now answers every tap: synced just now, already synced with the time the next sync opens, the provider asked us to slow down, or reconnect needed - no more spinner that goes quiet. Each account in Profile → Bank Connections shows the date of the bank's own data, and a balance that hasn't moved in two days is flagged as stale so you can tell a sleepy bridge from a bank that wants a fresh login. A connection that needs attention now also shows a banner on the Bridge and Budget tabs that opens the manager, with Later to hide it for the session.
  • Fix a stuck bank in one tap. When a connection is in trouble, Sync now skips the 15-minute cooldown so your retry after signing in at the bridge runs immediately (a provider that asked us to slow down still waits). And if the SimpleFIN Bridge stops accepting a connection, paste a new setup token right in the manager to reconnect it in place - your accounts, card links and imported history all stay.
  • The Bridge's Purchase Plans card is a glance again. It opens on just the combined progress bar - saved, still to go, and when everything funds - with the individual plans, ordering and monthly set-aside folded behind a Show plans tap. The Charts planning tool stays fully open.
  • The Review Inbox never loses a transaction silently anymore. Sync now says what it did beyond adding rows - how many it skipped and how many your rules auto-approved - and the inbox gets a Recently skipped list: every row that left without being approved in the last 30 days, with the reason (you skipped it, an Always Skip rule, your partner's phone, a match to something already reviewed, or a pending charge the bank stopped reporting) and a Restore button. A restored row returns to the inbox and is never skipped automatically again - only you decide what happens to it.

The calendar now reads the day exactly as it was saved.
  • Every day picker is a real calendar now. The recurring-bill day in the entry form and the custom due day in the debt form used to be plain blocks of 31 numbers; both now show the month's calendar with weekday headers and today outlined, so you can see at a glance that the 15th is a Tuesday. Days a shorter month doesn't have (the 29th to 31st) stay pickable, just dimmed, because the bill falls back to the last day as before. One calendar grid is shared by the Bill Calendar and all three pickers, so they can never drift apart again.

Bring In Your History

  • Import a bank statement. Download the transaction CSV from your bank's website (Profile → Data → Import Bank Statement), confirm which columns hold the date, description and amount - BudgetArk guesses, you check the preview - and every row lands in the Review Inbox to approve, categorize or skip, with your merchant rules applied. Ideal for months before you connected a bank, or a bank that has no connection at all. Layouts are remembered per bank, re-importing the same file never doubles anything, and rows that look like an entry you already have are flagged.
  • The Excel export is easier to read. Every column is sized to fit its contents (no more cut-off text or ##### amounts), each month's transactions on the Budget Entries sheet folds into a collapsible group so you can jump between months, and money columns line up with thousands separators. None of this changes the file's format - re-importing the workbook works exactly as before.
  • Review Inbox: your debts now appear in the 'Applies to bill' list. Open a card or loan payment that a bank import brought in and tap the debt - the amount is logged as a payment on the Debts tab (balance and history), the Budget counts it under Debt Payments, and no separate expense is filed, so the payment is never counted twice. Tick 'Always do this' and the merchant rule remembers the debt: future payments to that merchant (and any already waiting in the inbox) are logged on it without stopping for review, even when the bank text looks like a transfer. Rules can be pointed at a debt later from the inbox's Rules screen too. Paid-off debts stay out of the list. And it never double counts against the due-day reminder: if you already confirmed 'paid the minimum today' (or logged the payment on the Debts tab) and the bank's copy of that same payment arrives a few days later, the row is matched to the payment already on file and cleared without touching the balance - the inbox tells you so. A nearby hand-logged payment for a different amount is left for you to decide.
  • The Add / Edit Entry sheet's DAY picker is now a real calendar: weekday headers (Sun to Sat) with each date in its own column and today outlined, so you can tell Tuesday the 8th from Wednesday the 9th at a glance instead of counting along a strip of numbers. It follows the month you picked above it.
  • Review the inbox by vendor. When you have a lot to go through - a whole statement, or several months - tap 'Vendor' at the top of the Review Inbox to fold every transaction from the same store into one group, biggest first. Pick one category and approve the whole group at once, and tick 'Always file this vendor here' to have future imports sorted for you. 'Date' still groups by day as before.
September 2, 2026 v1.10.1

Banks That Keep Up, Plans That Add Up

  • Bank accounts can now land in any Bridge category. The '+ New account' form in the connection wizard (and in Profile → Bank Connections) offers 401k / Retirement and Investment alongside Checking, Savings, HSA and Other, and existing 401k or brokerage accounts appear in the 'Balance updates' picker too. A synced 401k balance shows as that account's Cash line on the Bridge; add its funds there as well and they count on top of it.
  • The form also guesses the category from the bank's own account name - 'Fidelity 401(k)' preselects 401k / Retirement, 'High Yield Savings' preselects Savings - instead of defaulting everything to Checking. It's only a default; tap another pill to change it.
  • Fixed: the connection wizard's 'Connected' screen could freeze the whole app on Done (force-close was the only way out). The first sync now waits until the wizard sheet is fully gone on both platforms.
  • Fixed: connecting a second phone to the same bank asked you to review transactions you had already reviewed on the first one. Anything you approved on one phone now clears from the other phone's Review Inbox as soon as the two sync, even if the second phone fetched the transactions first.
  • Skipped transactions now travel with partner sync too. The list of transactions you chose not to import is shared between your paired phones (only the 'skip' decisions - never bank credentials, and nothing about entries you've marked private), so the same charge isn't offered twice. Already-paired phones send their existing skips once, on the first sync after both update.
  • If one of your banks needs a fresh login on your SimpleFIN Bridge, BudgetArk now tells you. Some banks expire the bridge's session every couple of weeks; the app used to keep saying 'Last synced 2 hours ago' while that bank had quietly stopped. The connection now shows 'A bank needs attention on your SimpleFIN Bridge', and its detail screen lists the bridge's message with what to do (sign in at the bridge and reconnect the bank).
  • And when the bank comes back, the app catches up on its own. Before, a bank that was disconnected for three weeks only ever got its last week re-checked, so the transactions in between never appeared. The next sync after a reconnect now notices the gap and re-imports it (up to 90 days back). There's also a 'Re-import the last 90 days' button on each connection for good measure - anything you've already reviewed stays exactly as it is.
  • Your Purchase Plans now add up. The list on the Bridge (and in the Charts tool) opens with a summary: how much you've saved across every plan, how much is still to go, and the month everything is funded at your current pace.
  • Rank your plans the way you'd rank debts. Choose 'Smallest first' to knock out the cheap ones for momentum (the snowball), 'Soonest needed' to put the nearest need-by dates first, or 'My order' and move plans up and down yourself.
  • Then set one monthly amount for all of them. 'One at a time' pours it into the first plan and rolls the money into the next as each one is funded - exactly like a debt snowball - while 'Split evenly' shares it across every plan. Each plan shows when it'll be ready under that plan, what it gets this month, and a warning if it would miss its need-by date. The amount gets the same fits / tight / over check against your free cash flow as a single plan. Your ranking syncs, backs up, and round-trips through spreadsheets (new Priority column on the Savings Goals sheet).
  • Plan a Purchase now shows what a thing really costs. Type a price and the tool says how many hours of work it is at your take-home rate - worked out from the income you've logged and the hours you work per week, or an hourly figure you type - and puts financing it next to saving for it: the loan payment and total interest at an APR and term you pick, against how much later saving gets it and what you keep by waiting. If you're on an Ark step, it says plainly that a new loan moves you backwards.
